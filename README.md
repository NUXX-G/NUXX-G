> **A puzzle before the message.**
>
> The Latin alphabet contains 26 symbols. Somewhere in that cycle lives a single integer *n*, greater than zero, with a peculiar property: shift every letter forward by *n* positions, then shift the result forward by *n* again, and every letter lands back exactly where it started. No other positive value below 26 shares this property except its double, which brings you full circle in one step rather than two.
>
> Formally: find the smallest positive integer *n* such that
>
> `2n mod 26 = 0`, where `n ≠ 0` and `n ≠ 26`
>
> This same integer is prime, is not divisible by any number other than 1 and itself, and happens to mark the exact midpoint of the alphabet — the point where forward and backward shifts become indistinguishable.
>
> Once you have *n*, apply it as a shift to each letter below, wrapping from Z back to A. The technique predates modern cryptography by roughly two thousand years, though the specific shift value used here made it famous on early internet forums as a way to obscure spoilers and punchlines rather than actual secrets.
